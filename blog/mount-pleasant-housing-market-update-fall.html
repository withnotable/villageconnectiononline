What's Happening in the Mount Pleasant Housing Market This July

September 9th 2026

Every September, I get a wave of calls from people who are watching the Mount Pleasant market from the sidelines and are now ready to make a move. Some feel like they missed something. Others are wondering if fall is the right time to act. So let me give you a clear-eyed look at what I'm actually seeing on the ground right now.

Is Mount Pleasant a Buyer's Market or a Seller's Market Right Now?

At the moment, Mount Pleasant feels as if we are moving from a seller's market to more like balanced market territory. When comparing August of 2026 to Aug of 2025, the takeaway is: More homes sold, but buyers are paying less overall. We went from 131 to 136 closings, so the market is still moving. But the median sale price dropped 4.5%, from $1.086M to $1.038M. And that's a pretty good indication that buyers have more leverage than they did a year ago. Plus the days on market increased from 36 to 43.

What's Happening in Mount Pleasant Neighborhoods Right Now

Mount Pleasant is not one uniform market. What's happening in Hamlin Plantation looks different from what's happening in Ion or Old Village, so it's worth breaking this down a bit.

In Hamlin Plantation, I'm seeing steady interest from buyers drawn to the amenity-rich community and the mix of floor plans available. Homes that are well-maintained and priced accurately relative to recent sales are still generating solid activity.

In Ion, the New Urbanism design and walkable layout continue to attract buyers who want something architecturally distinct. Because the neighborhood has a limited number of homes, listings here tend to draw competitive attention when they're priced well.

In Old Village, the combination of Lowcountry character, mature trees, and proximity to Pitt Street Bridge keeps demand consistent. Most homes here are walkable to Shem Creek and the Pitt Street Shopping District, which adds a genuine lifestyle dimension that buyers in this price range respond to. Buyers in this pocket tend to be deliberate, and sellers who present their homes thoughtfully tend to do well even when the broader market softens.

In communities like Rivertowne on the Wando and Dunes West, there's been a noticeable uptick in move-up buyers, people already living in Mount Pleasant who are trading into larger homes or different configurations as their needs change.

What Does This Mean If You're Buying in Mount Pleasant Right Now?

If you're a buyer, the fall market in Mount Pleasant tends to be a little more breathable than the spring and summer rush, but that doesn't mean you should move slowly on a home you want. In desirable neighborhoods like Ion and Old Village, well-priced listings still attract multiple interested parties.

The most useful thing you can do right now is get your financing in order before you start touring homes seriously. Sellers pay attention to how clean an offer looks, and a pre-approval letter from a local lender carries weight. If you've been renting in Mount Pleasant or nearby and are ready to put down roots, it's worth having a conversation about what's realistically available in your budget right now rather than waiting for conditions that may or may not arrive.

What Does This Mean If You're Selling in Mount Pleasant Right Now?

Sellers who price their homes based on what comparable properties have actually sold for in the past 60 to 90 days are seeing better results than those who price based on what they hope the market will bear. That's true in every season, but it's especially true in July when buyers tend to be more deliberate.

If you're thinking about listing a home in Hamlin Plantation, Dunes West, or another established Mount Pleasant community, presentation still matters. Homes that are clean, uncluttered, and photographed well get more showings. More showings, combined with accurate pricing, give you the best realistic shot at a strong outcome. Jackie Kelly works with sellers to review local comparables before setting a price, so there's real data behind the number, not wishful thinking.

Two Things I'm Noticing on the Ground This Month

First, I'm seeing more contingent offers than I did six months ago. Buyers are a little more cautious, and more of them are asking for inspection contingencies and appraisal protections. If you're a seller, this doesn't mean your home won't sell well. It means your listing needs to be priced and presented in a way that doesn't leave buyers looking for reasons to renegotiate.

Second, the buyer pool in Mount Pleasant right now includes a meaningful number of people relocating from other parts of the country. They often have a narrow window to visit and make decisions, which means they're doing a lot of research online before they ever step foot in a neighborhood. If you're selling, your online listing is your first showing, and it needs to reflect the home accurately and attractively.
